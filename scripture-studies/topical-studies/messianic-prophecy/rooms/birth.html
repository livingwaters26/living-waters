Birth of the Messiah

Prophecies fulfilled in Jesus's conception, birth, and infancy

The earliest chapters of Matthew and Luke read almost like a checklist against centuries-old prophecy. This room gathers the Old Testament promises surrounding the Messiah's birth beside their New Testament fulfillments.

📖 Passages in Full
👼

1. Born of a virgin (Isaiah 7:14 → Matthew 1:22–23)

Prophecy
Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign: behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.
Fulfillment
Now all this is come to pass, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the Lord through the prophet, saying, Behold, the virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Immanuel; which is, being interpreted, God with us.
Isaiah's original Hebrew word (almah) can mean "young woman" as well as "virgin," which is the center of a long Jewish–Christian translation debate; Matthew is working from the Greek Septuagint's unambiguous parthenos, "virgin."
⭐

2. Born in Bethlehem (Micah 5:2 → Matthew 2:1, 5–6)

Prophecy
But thou, Beth-lehem Ephrathah, which art little to be among the thousands of Judah, out of thee shall one come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth are from of old, from everlasting.
Fulfillment
Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judaea... they said unto him, In Bethlehem of Judaea: for thus it is written through the prophet, And thou Bethlehem, land of Judah, art in no wise least among the princes of Judah: for out of thee shall come forth a governor, who shall be shepherd of my people Israel.
This is the rare case where first-century Jewish religious authorities and the Gospel writer agree completely on which verse the Messiah's birthplace prophecy is — the dispute in Matthew 2 is only over what Herod does with the answer.
🌟

3. A star and a scepter out of Jacob (Numbers 24:17 → Matthew 2:2, 9–10)

Prophecy
There shall come forth a star out of Jacob, and a sceptre shall rise out of Israel...
Fulfillment
Saying, Where is he that is born King of the Jews? for we saw his star in the east, and are come to worship him... and lo, the star, which they saw in the east, went before them, till it came and stood over where the young child was.
Balaam was himself a Gentile seer from the east (Numbers 22–24), which many read as adding resonance to Gentile magi being the ones who recognize "his star."
🇪🇬

4. Called out of Egypt (Hosea 11:1 → Matthew 2:14–15)

Prophecy
When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt.
Fulfillment
And he arose and took the young child and his mother by night, and departed into Egypt... that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the Lord through the prophet, saying, Out of Egypt did I call my son.
Hosea's original statement is a historical look backward at the nation of Israel, not a forward-looking prediction — Matthew's use is a classic case of typological fulfillment (Jesus recapitulating Israel's story), not direct verbal prophecy, and traditions differ on how to categorize that kind of "fulfillment."
😭

5. Rachel weeping for her children (Jeremiah 31:15 → Matthew 2:16–18)

Prophecy
Thus saith Jehovah: A voice is heard in Ramah, lamentation, and bitter weeping, Rachel weeping for her children; she refuseth to be comforted for her children, because they are not.
Fulfillment
Then was fulfilled that which was spoken through Jeremiah the prophet, saying, A voice was heard in Ramah, weeping and great mourning, Rachel weeping for her children; and she would not be comforted, because they are not.
In Jeremiah's own context this verse is immediately followed by a promise of return and hope (31:16–17) — Matthew's readers steeped in the passage would have caught that unstated hopeful continuation even in the horror of the massacre.
📯

6. A voice crying in the wilderness (Isaiah 40:3; Malachi 3:1 → Matthew 3:1–3)

Prophecy
The voice of one that crieth, Prepare ye in the wilderness the way of Jehovah... Behold, I send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me.
Fulfillment
In those days cometh John the Baptist, preaching in the wilderness of Judaea... For this is he that was spoken of through Isaiah the prophet, saying, The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Make ye ready the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.
All four Gospels apply this same Isaiah text to John the Baptist — one of the most theologically uncontested Old Testament identifications in the entire New Testament.
👑

7. Heir to David's eternal throne (2 Samuel 7:12–13; Isaiah 9:6–7 → Luke 1:32–33)

Prophecy
I will set up thy seed after thee... and I will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever... Of the increase of his government and of peace there shall be no end, upon the throne of David, and upon his kingdom.
Fulfillment
He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Most High: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: and he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.
This is the single clearest textual bridge between the Davidic covenant and the New Testament's announcement of Jesus's birth — see also the Reign room, where this same promise is treated at length.

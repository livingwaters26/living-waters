Passion of the Messiah

Prophecies fulfilled in the events leading to the cross

"Passion" here covers the final week: the Triumphal Entry, the betrayal, and the arrest — the sequence of events immediately surrounding Jesus's suffering, each anticipated centuries in advance in striking detail.

📖 Passages in Full
🐴

1. The King comes lowly, on a donkey (Zechariah 9:9 → Matthew 21:4–5)

Prophecy
Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy king cometh unto thee; he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, even upon a colt the foal of an ass.
Fulfillment
Now this is come to pass, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken through the prophet, saying, Tell ye the daughter of Zion, Behold, thy King cometh unto thee, Meek, and riding upon an ass...
Zechariah 9:9–10 pairs this humble arrival with worldwide, weapon-abolishing peace (9:10) — a tension every tradition has to address, since the peace clearly didn't arrive at the Triumphal Entry itself.
🤝

2. Betrayed by a close friend (Psalm 41:9 → John 13:18; Matthew 26:23)

Prophecy
Yea, mine own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, who did eat of my bread, Hath lifted up his heel against me.
Fulfillment
I speak not of you all... but that the scripture may be fulfilled, He that eateth my bread lifted up his heel against me.
Jesus quotes this verse Himself, applying David's ancient complaint to His own imminent betrayal — a rare instance of Jesus directly citing His own fulfillment of a specific psalm before the event occurs.
🪙

3. Thirty pieces of silver (Zechariah 11:12–13 → Matthew 26:15; 27:3–10)

Prophecy
So they weighed for my hire thirty pieces of silver... And Jehovah said unto me, Cast it unto the potter, the goodly price that I was prized at by them.
Fulfillment
And said, What are ye willing to give me, and I will deliver him unto you? And they weighed unto him thirty pieces of silver... and gave them for the potter's field, as the Lord appointed me.
Matthew 27:9 attributes this citation to "Jeremiah," though the wording is from Zechariah — most interpreters across traditions explain this via an ancient scroll-naming convention (the Prophets' scroll was sometimes headed by Jeremiah) or a blending with Jeremiah 19 and 32's own potter/field imagery.
🐑

4. Struck shepherd, scattered sheep (Zechariah 13:7 → Matthew 26:31, 56)

Prophecy
Awake, O sword, against my shepherd... smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered.
Fulfillment
Then saith Jesus unto them, All ye shall be offended in me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered abroad... Then all the disciples left him, and fled.
Jesus explicitly frames His own disciples' coming failure as itself a scripted fulfillment of prophecy, not merely a personal moral lapse — a detail every tradition wrestles with regarding divine sovereignty and human responsibility together.
🤫

5. Silent before his accusers (Isaiah 53:7 → Matthew 27:12–14)

Prophecy
He was oppressed, yet when he was afflicted he opened not his mouth; as a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and as a sheep that before its shearers is dumb, so he opened not his mouth.
Fulfillment
And when he was accused by the chief priests and elders, he answered nothing... he gave him no answer, not even to one word: insomuch that the governor marvelled greatly.
This silence is deliberately contrasted, across all four Gospels, with Jesus's readiness to speak plainly at other points in His trial (e.g. Matt 26:64) — the silence is selective, not blanket, which shapes how each tradition reads its significance.

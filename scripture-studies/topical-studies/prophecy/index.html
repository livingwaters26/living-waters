Prophecy & Apocalyptic Literature

How to read the Bible's visions, symbols, and end-times discourse as their own genre — not as either a code to crack or a story to shrug off.

🔀 Not a duplicate of Millennial Kingdom Prophecy. That module tracks every kingdom-related passage across all sixteen prophets and compares premil/amil/postmil readings verse by verse. This module is broader and one level up: it's about how to read apocalyptic literature as a genre at all — the visions of Daniel and Ezekiel, Jesus's own end-times discourse, and Revelation's symbolic vocabulary — regardless of which millennial view you land on. See Millennial Kingdom Prophecy for the kingdom-passage-by-passage work.
Four reading strategies come up constantly in this literature — not just for the millennium, but for symbols, timelines, and "when" language generally: Preterist (fulfilled in the past, usually by 70 AD), Historicist (unfolding across church history), Idealist (timeless symbolic truth, not pinned to specific events), and Futurist (still substantially ahead). Most real interpreters blend more than one — a full-preterist Revelation reading and a partial-preterist one are quite different positions.

PATRIARCHS

Period 2 of 12 · Genesis 12–50

Summary

After the scattering at Babel, God begins gathering a family for himself again — this time by calling one man. Genesis 12–50 follows four generations: Abraham, his son Isaac, his grandson Jacob (renamed Israel), and Jacob's twelve sons, especially Joseph. Where Genesis 1–11 covered the whole human race in broad strokes, this period narrows in on one family through whom God promises to bless "all the families of the earth" (Genesis 12:3).

Narrative

God calls Abram out of Ur with a promise: land, descendants, and blessing for the whole world through him (Genesis 12:1–3). Abram's faith is tested repeatedly — famine, a childless old age, and finally the command to offer his long-awaited son Isaac on Mount Moriah (Genesis 22), a passage the Church has long read as a foreshadowing of the Father offering his own Son.

The promise passes to Isaac, then to his son Jacob, whose life is marked by deception, wrestling (both with his brother Esau and, at Peniel, with God himself), and the birth of twelve sons who become the twelve tribes of Israel. The period closes with Joseph — sold into slavery by his own brothers, exalted in Egypt, and ultimately the means by which his whole family is preserved from famine, setting the stage for Israel's thriving in Egypt at the start of the next period.

Key Events

1
The Call of AbramGenesis 12:1–9
God calls Abram to leave his homeland for a land he will show him, promising to make him a great nation and to bless all nations through him — the covenant that shapes everything that follows in Scripture.
2
The Sacrifice of IsaacGenesis 22:1–19
God tests Abraham by commanding him to offer Isaac; Abraham's obedience and God's provision of a ram in Isaac's place have long been read as pointing ahead to the Father and the Son.
3
Jacob Wrestles at PenielGenesis 32:22–32
Jacob wrestles with a divine figure through the night and receives a new name, Israel — "he who strives with God" — the name that will belong to the whole nation descended from him.
4
Joseph Sold into EgyptGenesis 37:12–36
Jacob's sons sell their brother Joseph into slavery out of jealousy — the very act God will use to preserve the whole family through the famine still to come.
5
Israel Settles in EgyptGenesis 46:1–47:12
Reunited with Joseph, Jacob and his whole household relocate to Egypt's fertile land of Goshen — seventy persons who will grow, over four centuries, into a nation.

EARLY WORLD

Period 1 of 12 · Genesis 1–11

Summary

The Bible opens with a grand overture that prepares for all that is to follow (Genesis 1–11). God creates the world, including human beings as the crown of creation. But their disobedience leads to a series of tragic consequences, including the first murder, a primeval flood, and the scattering of peoples at Babel. Genesis does not aim to give a scientific explanation of the origins of the universe; rather, it uses the form of ancient narrative to teach who God is, who we are, and how sin entered a good creation.

Narrative

In Genesis 1, God speaks and brings all things into being. He creates an ordered and good world and makes human beings in his own image and likeness (Genesis 1:27). Genesis 2 retells creation from a different vantage point: while chapter 1 accentuates God's majesty as Creator of all, chapter 2 focuses on God's relation to humanity, placing Adam and Eve in the garden in fellowship with him.

The serpent enters the scene in Genesis 3, casting doubt on God's goodness and tempting Adam and Eve to disobey the one command they were given. Their fall brings shame, estrangement from God, and death into the world — but also the first promise of a Redeemer, the protoevangelium of Genesis 3:15.

Genesis 4 records the tragedy of Cain and Abel, humanity's sin spreading from the individual to relationships between brothers. By Genesis 6, "the wickedness of man was great in the earth," and God sends the Flood — while preserving Noah's family through the ark as a picture of judgment and mercy held together. After the waters recede, God establishes a covenant with Noah, promising never again to destroy the earth by flood (Genesis 9).

The period closes with Babel (Genesis 11), where humanity's pride in building a tower to make a name for itself results in confused languages and scattered peoples — setting the stage for God's next move: calling one man, Abram, to begin gathering a family for himself again.

Key Events

1
CreationGenesis 1:1–2:3
By speaking, God creates "the heavens and the earth" — all that exists, spiritual and material. Brought forth from nothing, all creatures depend on God for their very existence.
2
The FallGenesis 3:1–24
The serpent tempts Adam and Eve by casting doubt on God's goodness, and they disobey, inaugurating the tragic history of sin and its consequences for the whole human family.
3
Curse and PromiseGenesis 3:8–24
Pain and laborious work enter human experience. Yet in the middle of judgment, God gives the first promise of redemption, pointing ahead to the One who will crush the serpent's head.
4
The FloodGenesis 6:5–9:17
God judges a world given over to violence, but preserves Noah's family through the ark, then establishes a covenant with all creation, sealed by the rainbow.
5
People Scattered at BabelGenesis 11:1–9
Humanity's pride, building a tower "to make a name for ourselves," results in confused languages and scattered nations — the backdrop against which God calls Abram in Genesis 12.

Covenant Structure of Salvation History

This period establishes the first link in a chain of covenants that runs through the whole Bible — each one widening the family God is calling to himself.

Adam · One Holy Couple Noah · One Holy Family Abraham · One Holy Tribe Moses · One Holy Nation David · One Holy Kingdom Jesus · One Holy Church

Understanding the Colors on This Page

Two separate color systems are in use across Living Waters — each with its own reasoning, kept visually distinct so they never compete.

1. The 12 Periods of Salvation History

Adopted from the Great Adventure Bible Timeline — each period's color was chosen for a specific reason tied to what happens in it, not arbitrarily:

Early World — turquoise, the color of the earth seen from space
Patriarchs — burgundy, God's blood covenant with Abraham
Egypt & Exodus — red, the Red Sea
Desert Wanderings — tan, the color of the desert
Conquest & Judges — green, the hills of Canaan
Royal Kingdom — purple, the color of royalty
Divided Kingdom — black, Israel's darkening period
Exile — baby blue, Judah "singing the blues" in Babylon
Return — yellow, returning home to brighter days
Maccabean Revolt — orange, fire in the Temple's purified lamps
Messianic Fulfillment — gold, the gifts of the Magi
The Church — white, the spotless Bride of Christ
2. Inline Context-Clue Coloring (in the reading text)

Within the running scripture text itself, individual words are colored to answer the questions you'd naturally ask of any passage — who, what, where, when, and where is God in this:

God — deep gold, echoing the "Messianic/glory" gold above; every divine reference stands out at a glance
Time — teal-blue, chosen to feel like the steady rhythm of days and seasons passing
People — blue, matching the site's own sky-blue accent color used for links and navigation
Place — green, the color of land and geography, same reasoning as "Conquest & Judges" above

The two systems are kept visually separate on purpose: the period badge is always a single pill-shaped tag at the top of a passage, while context-clue coloring only ever touches individual words inline — so they layer without competing.

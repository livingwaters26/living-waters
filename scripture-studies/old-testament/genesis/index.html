✍️ Author Profile — Genesis

Who wrote it, when, and to whom.

Identity
Traditionally attributed to Moses (Mosaic authorship), the view held by Jewish tradition and the majority of church history; historical-critical scholarship outside this study's six traditions proposes composite, later sources — a debate the traditions compared here generally do not treat as settled against Mosaic authorship.
Time Period
Content spans primordial history through roughly 1800s BC (the patriarchs); traditionally composed by Moses during Israel's wilderness wanderings, c. 15th-13th century BC depending on the dating of the Exodus.
Target Audience
The nation of Israel, particularly the generation preparing to enter Canaan — establishing their origins, their covenant identity, and their relationship to the one true God against the backdrop of Egypt's and Canaan's polytheism.
Historical Context — Empires & Era
No Israelite king yet exists in this period. The patriarchal narratives sit within the broader Ancient Near Eastern world of the Sumerian/Akkadian and Old Babylonian periods (Abraham) and Egypt's Middle Kingdom and Second Intermediate Period (Joseph).
📌 All 7 chapter groupings built — Creation (1-2), The Fall (3), the Flood and Noah's Covenant (6-9), the Abrahamic Covenant (12, 15, 17), Sodom and Gomorrah (18-19), the Binding of Isaac (22), and the Joseph Narrative (37-50): the passages most theologically load-bearing and most cross-tradition-divergent in the book.

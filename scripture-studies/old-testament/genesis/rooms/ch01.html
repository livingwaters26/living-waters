ICultural & Historical Context+

Written into a world of competing creation myths — Babylonian, Egyptian, Canaanite — Genesis 1 makes a quiet, radical claim: one God, speaking rather than warring his way into existence, and a cosmos that is orderly, good, and purposeful rather than the leftover battlefield of rival deities.

IICore Theological Meaning+

The seven-day structure moves from forming to filling — light and space first, then what inhabits them — culminating not in humanity's creation but in the Sabbath rest that follows it. Being made "in his image" (1:27) grounds human dignity in something given, not earned.

IIICross-References & Canonical Links+

John 1:1–3 reopens Genesis with the Word present at creation. 2 Corinthians 4:6 reuses "let there be light" as the pattern for new-creation illumination in the heart. Revelation 21:1 closes the canon where this page opens it — a new heavens and a new earth.

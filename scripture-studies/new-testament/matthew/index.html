The Gospel of Matthew

Verse-by-verse, compared across six traditions: where they agree, where they diverge, and why. Non-denominational layer sourced from VerseByVerseMinistry.org (Steve Armstrong), with C.I. Scofield sprinkled in; the Baptist layer also draws on John Gill alongside Spurgeon.

✍️ Author Profile — Matthew

Who wrote it, when, and to whom.

Identity
Levi/Matthew, a Jewish tax collector (publican) called directly by Jesus (Matt 9:9); one of the Twelve. Early church tradition (Papias, c. 100-140 AD; Irenaeus) attributes this Gospel to him.
Time Period
Most scholars date the Gospel to roughly 70-90 AD; a substantial conservative minority argues for a pre-70 AD date. Papias records an early tradition of a Hebrew/Aramaic form preceding the Greek text we have.
Target Audience
Primarily a Jewish-Christian readership — the Gospel assumes familiarity with Jewish customs, argues Jesus's credentials as Messiah and Son of David from the very first verse, and is saturated with Old Testament fulfillment citations more than any other Gospel.
Historical Context — Empire & Era
Written under the Roman Empire, likely in the shadow of the Jewish-Roman War (66-73 AD) and the Temple's destruction (70 AD) if the majority dating holds; emperors of the era include Nero, Vespasian, Titus, and Domitian. The church was still closely, and increasingly painfully, entangled with the Jewish community it was emerging from.

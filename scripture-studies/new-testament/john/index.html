✍️ Author Profile — John

Who wrote it, when, and to whom.

Identity
The Apostle John, son of Zebedee, one of the Twelve and part of Jesus's innermost circle (with Peter and James); traditionally identified as "the disciple whom Jesus loved" who appears repeatedly in this Gospel's narrative.
Time Period
Widely dated to roughly 85-95 AD, likely the last-written of the four Gospels, from Ephesus according to early church tradition (Irenaeus).
Target Audience
A broader, more explicitly evangelistic and universal audience than Matthew's — the Gospel states its own purpose directly: "these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God" (20:31), explaining Jewish customs for readers who may not share them.
Historical Context — Empire & Era
Written under Roman emperors Domitian or Nerva, decades after Jerusalem's fall (70 AD), likely amid growing tension between the church and the synagogue as Christianity became more clearly distinct from Judaism.

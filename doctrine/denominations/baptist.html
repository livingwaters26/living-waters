Baptist

Congregations defined by believer's baptism and radical local-church independence

📋 Quick Facts

Key Figures
John Smyth and Thomas Helwys, English Separatist exiles in Amsterdam
Formative Era
Early 1600s in the Netherlands and England, through the 1689 London Baptist Confession
Governance
Congregational — each local church is fully self-governing, with no binding external denominational hierarchy
Key Documents
The 1689 London Baptist Confession (among several Baptist confessions across different branches)

🕰️ Historical Origin

Baptist origins trace to English Separatists — Christians who believed the Church of England itself needed to be left, not merely reformed from within, unlike most other Reformation movements. John Smyth, exiled to Amsterdam, baptized himself and then his followers by affusion around 1609 in the conviction that only believers old enough for genuine faith should be baptized, rejecting infant baptism entirely. Thomas Helwys, Smyth's colleague, returned to England and founded the first Baptist church on English soil around 1612, also becoming an early and forceful advocate for full religious liberty — including for non-Christians — an unusually radical position for the era.

Baptist churches split early into Particular Baptists (holding to Calvinist particular/limited atonement) and General Baptists (holding to a more Arminian general atonement) — a divide that persists in different forms today. The 1689 London Baptist Confession, closely modeled on the Westminster Confession but revised for believer's baptism and congregational governance, became the most influential Calvinistic Baptist confessional statement, still referenced by many Reformed Baptist churches.

✦ Core Distinctives

Believer's Baptism by Immersion

Only professing believers old enough for genuine faith should be baptized, and only by full immersion — rejecting infant baptism as lacking biblical warrant.

Local Church Autonomy

Each congregation governs itself independently — calling its own pastor, interpreting Scripture, and setting its own practice, without binding denominational hierarchy above it.

Regenerate Church Membership

Church membership is reserved for those who can credibly profess personal faith — the church is meant to be a gathered community of believers, not a whole geographic parish.

Soul Liberty

A historic Baptist emphasis on individual freedom of conscience in religious matters, free from state or church coercion — traceable to Helwys's early advocacy and later figures like Roger Williams.

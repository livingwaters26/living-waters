Denominations Compared

Eleven traditions, five core questions, presented side by side — each tradition's own strongest self-understanding, not a ranking.

🌳 How these traditions branched: the 1054 Great Schism split Rome from Constantinople (East/West). The 1517 Reformation split Western Christianity into Catholic and Protestant, with Protestantism itself fragmenting quickly — Lutheran (1520s), Reformed/Calvinist (1530s-40s), and the Radical Reformation's Anabaptists (1525) all emerged within a generation of each other. Anglicanism split from Rome separately (1534, Henry VIII) and later produced Methodism as an internal revival movement (1730s) that became its own denomination after Wesley's death. Baptists emerged from English Separatism (early 1600s). Pentecostalism is the newest major branch (Azusa Street, 1906), emerging out of the Holiness movement within Methodism. See the full visual family tree and this same story in more depth at Church History & Doctrinal Development.

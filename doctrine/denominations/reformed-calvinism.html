Reformed / Calvinism

The tradition shaped by John Calvin's Geneva, defined by God's sovereignty in salvation

📋 Quick Facts

Key Figure
John Calvin, French reformer based in Geneva; also Huldrych Zwingli in Zurich as an earlier, independent Reformed voice
Formative Era
1530s-40s in Geneva, through the 1618-19 Synod of Dort
Governance
Presbyterian — elder-led (Greek presbyteros), with connected regional and national assemblies
Key Documents
Calvin's Institutes of the Christian Religion, the Westminster Confession (1646), the Canons of Dort

🕰️ Historical Origin

The Reformed tradition began independently of Luther, with Huldrych Zwingli's reforms in Zurich in the 1520s, but is most associated with John Calvin, a French lawyer-turned-theologian who settled in Geneva and wrote the Institutes of the Christian Religion — the movement's most systematic theological statement, expanded across several editions from 1536 to 1559. Calvin emphasized God's sovereignty across every area of life, not only salvation, and worked to reshape Geneva's civic and church life together.

The Synod of Dort (1618-19), convened in the Netherlands to address the theology of Jacobus Arminius and his followers (the Remonstrants), produced the five points later organized into the acronym TULIP — the tradition's most famous doctrinal summary, though Calvin himself never used that framework. The Westminster Confession (1646), drafted in England during the Civil War era, became the standard confessional document for Presbyterian churches worldwide.

✦ Core Distinctives

TULIP

Total depravity, Unconditional election, Limited atonement, Irresistible grace, Perseverance of the saints — the five points formalized at Dort in response to Arminian objections.

Covenant Theology

Scripture's whole storyline is read through a covenant of works (with Adam) and a covenant of grace (fulfilled in Christ) — a unifying interpretive framework distinctive to this tradition.

Presbyterian Governance

Churches are led by elected elders (a session), connected through regional presbyteries and national general assemblies — a middle structure between episcopal hierarchy and pure congregational independence.

Regulative Principle of Worship

Public worship should include only what Scripture explicitly commands or clearly implies, historically producing simpler, less ceremonial worship than Catholic, Orthodox, or Anglican practice.

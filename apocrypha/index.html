Deuterocanonical

The Apocrypha

The deuterocanonical / intertestamental books, and how three traditions view them

🕰️ Quick history: these books were part of the Greek Septuagint the early church actually used, and stayed in most Bibles — including the original 1611 KJV — for over a millennium. Jerome (~400 AD) was uneasy about them since they weren't in the Hebrew canon, but Augustine's wider view won out for centuries. Trent formally declared them canonical for Catholics in 1546, directly responding to the Reformation. Protestant Bible societies began dropping them from printings in the 1820s. Full timeline coming to this room.
Planned framing: Catholic (fully canonical, "deuterocanonical" — defined at Trent), Eastern Orthodox (an even broader canon, varying somewhat by jurisdiction), and Protestant (not canonical — but distinguishing Luther's own view, that these books are "not equal to Scripture but profitable and good to read," from the later Reformed/Puritan position that dropped them from printings entirely). Books to be covered: Tobit, Judith, 1–2 Maccabees, Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach (Ecclesiasticus), Baruch, and the additions to Esther and Daniel, at minimum.

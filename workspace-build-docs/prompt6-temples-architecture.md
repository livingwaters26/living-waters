## 2. Structural Breakdown Per Sanctuary

### 2.1 Mosaic Tabernacle
- **Outer Court:** 100 x 50 cubits (~150 x 75 ft), linen-curtained perimeter. Contains the Bronze (Brazen) Altar and the Bronze Laver.
- **Holy Place:** 20 x 10 cubits. Contains the Golden Lampstand (Menorah), Table of Showbread, Altar of Incense.
- **Most Holy Place (Holy of Holies):** 10 x 10 cubits, cube-shaped. Contains the Ark of the Covenant, entered once yearly by the High Priest on Yom Kippur.
- **Access gradient:** court (all Israel) → Holy Place (priests only) → Most Holy Place (High Priest, once/year) — a structural theology of graduated access to God's presence.

### 2.2 Solomon's Temple (First Temple)
- **Overall structure:** roughly double the Tabernacle's dimensions — Holy Place 40 x 20 cubits, Most Holy Place 20 x 20 x 20 cubits (still cubic).
- **Additions beyond the Tabernacle:** two free-standing bronze pillars at the entrance (Jachin and Boaz), ten lampstands and ten showbread tables (vs. one each in the Tabernacle), the massive Bronze Sea and ten portable lavers, side chambers built into the outer walls.
- **Ark placement:** Most Holy Place, beneath two large olive-wood cherubim (15 ft wingspan each) — the Tabernacle's smaller cherubim on the mercy seat remained inside.
- **Destroyed:** 586 BC by Nebuchadnezzar (2 Kings 25:9).

### 2.3 Zerubbabel's / Ezra's Temple (Second Temple, original phase)
- **Context:** built by returning exiles under Zerubbabel and Joshua the priest, completed 516 BC, prompted/pressured by Haggai and Zechariah's preaching.
- **Notably absent:** the Ark of the Covenant — lost/hidden at the Babylonian destruction and never present in the Second Temple in any of its phases. The Most Holy Place stood empty.
- **Scale:** modest enough that older Israelites who remembered Solomon's Temple wept at the foundation-laying (Ezra 3:12) — later Herodian expansion addressed this directly.

### 2.4 Herod's Temple Expansion
- **Scope:** not a new temple but a massive expansion/renovation of the Second Temple, begun c. 20 BC, still under construction during Jesus' ministry (John 2:20) and only fully finished c. AD 63 — seven years before its destruction.
- **Additions:** the Court of the Gentiles (outermost court, where Jesus overturned tables — Matt. 21:12), Court of Women, Court of Israel, Court of Priests, all arranged in concentric rings of increasing restriction toward the sanctuary itself. The retaining walls (part of which survive as the Western/"Wailing" Wall) enclosed a vastly expanded platform.
- **Destroyed:** AD 70 by Roman forces under Titus, fulfilling Jesus' prediction in Matthew 24:2.

### 2.5 Ezekiel's Visionary Temple (Ezekiel 40–48)
- **Nature:** given in exquisite measured detail (Ezekiel 40:5 onward, with a measuring reed) but never built — its status (literal future temple, symbolic vision, or both) is a genuinely disputed question across interpretive traditions, and the module should present it as such rather than picking a single reading.
- **Distinctive features:** a river flowing from the threshold that deepens progressively and brings life/healing wherever it flows (Ezekiel 47:1–12); a return of "the glory of the LORD" into the temple (Ezekiel 43:1–5), reversing the departure of glory earlier in the book (Ezekiel 10).
- **Interpretive options to present neutrally:** (a) a literal future millennial temple, (b) an idealized symbolic vision of restored worship, (c) typologically fulfilled in Christ and the Church rather than a physical structure. This aligns with the interpretive spectrum already represented in the site's Millennial Kingdom topic module.

## 5. Priestly Duties & Sacrificial System Summary

- **High Priest:** sole access to the Most Holy Place, once yearly on Yom Kippur (Leviticus 16); wore the ephod and breastpiece bearing the twelve tribes.
- **Priests (Aaronic line):** daily Holy Place duties — tending the lampstand, replacing showbread weekly, offering incense.
- **Levites:** broader logistical/support service, transport (in the Tabernacle era), and later temple maintenance/music.
- **Sacrificial categories:** burnt offering (total consecration), sin offering (atonement for unintentional sin), guilt offering (restitution), grain offering (devotion), fellowship/peace offering (communion) — each typologically converges on Christ's singular sacrifice per Hebrews' argument.
